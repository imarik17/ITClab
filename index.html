Input Tax Credit (ITC) under GST

Input Tax Credit is the backbone of the Goods and Services Tax system, designed to mitigate the **cascading effect of taxes** (tax on tax) and facilitate a seamless flow of credit across the supply chain.

1 Core Concept & Cascading Tax Elimination

Understanding how ITC allows businesses to set off output tax liabilities against taxes paid on procurements.

What is Input Tax Credit?

Under Section 2(62) of the CGST Act, **"Input Tax"** in relation to a registered person means the Central Tax (CGST), State Tax (SGST), Integrated Tax (IGST), or Union Territory Tax (UTGST) charged on any supply of goods or services made to him.

Fundamental Principle

A registered taxable person pays tax only on the Value Added at their stage, as credit for tax paid at the previous stage is available to set off against output liability.

Without ITC vs With ITC

Pre-GST / Without Credit (Cascading Tax)

Tax paid on inputs becomes part of the product cost. Subsequent tax is calculated on the total cost including previous tax $\rightarrow$ Tax on Tax.

Under GST / With ITC (Continuous Chain)

Tax paid on inputs is claimed as credit in the Electronic Credit Ledger. Net GST payable = Output Tax minus Input Tax Credit.

2 Key Statutory Provisions (CGST Act, 2017)

Detailed analysis of Sections 16, 17, 18, 49, and Section 16(4) time limits.

Section 16(1) - Fundamental Right to Credit

Every registered person shall be entitled to take credit of input tax charged on any supply of goods or services which are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of his business.

The 5 Cumulative Conditions for Availing ITC [Section 16(2)]

1 Tax Invoice Possession

Must possess a tax invoice, debit note, or prescribed tax-paying document issued by a registered supplier under Section 16(2)(a).

2 Receipt of Goods/Services

Goods or services must have been received by the registered person [Section 16(2)(b)]. Deemed receipt applicable for Bill-to-Ship-to model.

3 Supplier Tax Payment

Tax charged in respect of supply has been actually paid to the Government (either via cash or ITC utilization) under Section 16(2)(c).

4 Filing of Returns

The registered person must have furnished the return under Section 39 (Form GSTR-3B) under Section 16(2)(d).

5 GSTR-2B Matching Sec 16(2)(aa)

Details of invoice/debit note furnished by supplier in GSTR-1 and communicated to recipient in GSTR-2B (Strict 100% matching mandatory).

180-Day Payment Rule

2nd Proviso to Sec 16(2): If recipient fails to pay supplier value + tax within 180 days from invoice date, ITC must be reversed with interest @18% p.a.

Section 17: Apportionment of Credit & Common Credits

Section 17(1) - Business vs Personal Use

Where goods or services are used partly for business and partly for other (personal) purposes, ITC is restricted to what is attributable to business purposes.

Section 17(2) - Taxable vs Exempt Supplies

Where goods or services are used partly for taxable (including zero-rated) and partly for exempt supplies, credit is available ONLY for taxable supplies.

Rule 42 & 43: Common Credit Reversal Mechanism

Rule 42 (Inputs & Input Services): Common credit attributable to exempt supplies ($D_1 = (E/F) \times C_2$) and non-business purposes ($D_2 = 5\% \times C_2$) must be added to output tax liability monthly in GSTR-3B.

Rule 43 (Capital Goods): Credit on capital goods used commonly is amortized over a useful life of 60 months (5 years) and reversed proportionally for exempt turnover each month.

Section 18: Special Circumstances for ITC Availability

Sec 18(1)(a) - New Registration

Person applying for GST within 30 days of becoming liable can claim ITC on inputs held in stock, semi-finished, and finished goods on day prior to liability.

Sec 18(1)(c) - Switching from Composition to Regular

Entitled to credit on inputs in stock and capital goods (reduced by 5% per quarter from invoice date) on day prior to becoming regular taxpayer.

Sec 18(1)(d) - Exempt Supply Becoming Taxable

Entitled to ITC on inputs in stock and capital goods (reduced by 5% per quarter) used exclusively for such supply on preceding day.

Sec 18(4) - Reversal on Switching to Composition

When regular taxpayer shifts to composition scheme or taxable supply becomes exempt, ITC in stock/capital goods must be paid back in cash or reversed.

Section 16(4) - Time Limit for Availing Input Tax Credit

A registered person cannot claim ITC in respect of any invoice or debit note after the 30th day of November following the end of the financial year to which such invoice/debit note pertains OR furnishing of the relevant Annual Return (GSTR-9), whichever is earlier.

Timeline Example (FY 2023-24 Invoices):

Invoice Date: 15th January 2024 (FY 23-24) Cut-off Date: 30th November 2024 (in GSTR-3B of Oct/Nov)

Note: Finance Act amendments aligned the timeline to 30th November (previously due date of September return).

3 Blocked Credits - Section 17(5)

Ineligible ITCs notwithstanding anything contained in Section 16(1) or Section 18(1).

Sec 17(5)(a)

Motor Vehicles

Motor vehicles for transportation of persons having approved seating capacity of $\le$ 13 persons (including driver).

Exceptions (Allowed if): Used for further supply of such vehicles, transportation of passengers, or imparting driving training.
Sec 17(5)(b)(i)

Food, Catering & Health

Food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery, life/health insurance.

Exceptions: Allowed if used as element of taxable composite supply or where obligatory for employer under law.
Sec 17(5)(b)(ii)

Club & Fitness Memberships

Membership of a club, health, and fitness centre for employees or management.

Exceptions: Allowed only if statutory obligation on employer to provide same to employees under any law.
Sec 17(5)(c) & (d)

Immovable Property Construction

Works contract services or goods/services received for construction of immovable property (on own account or business).

Exceptions: Allowed for Plant & Machinery, or where works contract is input for further works contract service.
Sec 17(5)(g)

Personal Consumption

Goods or services or both used for personal consumption of proprietor, partners, or directors.

No Exceptions: Strictly prohibited. Must be reversed if initially claimed.
Sec 17(5)(h)

Lost, Written Off or Free Gifts

Goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off, or disposed of by way of gift or free samples.

Note: Promotional schemes like "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" follow composite/mixed supply rules rather than direct 17(5)(h) blocking.

4 Order of ITC Utilization (Rule 88A)

Mandatory statutory hierarchy for setting off Input Tax Credit against GST Output Liabilities.

Golden Rule of Utilization:

IGST Credit must be completely exhausted first before using CGST or SGST credit. Once IGST credit is zero, IGST liability (if any remaining) can be paid by CGST, then SGST. Cross-utilization between CGST and SGST is STRICTLY PROHIBITED.

ITC Credit Type ↓ 1st Set-off Output 2nd Set-off Output 3rd Set-off Output
IGST Credit IGST (100% first) CGST / SGST (In ANY ratio or proportion at taxpayer's option)
CGST Credit CGST IGST SGST (BLOCKED )
SGST / UTGST Credit SGST / UTGST IGST CGST (BLOCKED )

Case 2: Rule 42 Common Credit Reversal Calculation

Common Credit Formula
Given Monthly Turnover Data:
  • Total Turnover ($F$): ₹1,00,00,000
  • Exempt Supplies Turnover ($E$): ₹20,00,000 (20%)
  • Taxable Supplies Turnover: ₹80,00,000 (80%)
  • Total Input Tax Credit ($T$): ₹5,00,000
Credit Breakup ($T$):
  • $T_1$ (Exclusively personal): ₹20,000
  • $T_2$ (Exclusively exempt): ₹30,000
  • $T_3$ (Blocked Sec 17(5)): ₹50,000
  • $T_4$ (Exclusively taxable): ₹3,000,000
Step-by-Step Mathematical Calculation:
  1. Common Credit $C_2 = T - (T_1 + T_2 + T_3 + T_4) = 5,00,000 - 4,00,000 = \mathbf{₹1,00,000}$
  2. Exempt Proportion Reversal ($D_1$) = $(E / F) \times C_2 = (20,00,000 / 1,00,00,000) \times 1,00,000 = \mathbf{₹20,000}$
  3. Non-business Reversal ($D_2$) = $5\% \times C_2 = 5\% \times 1,00,000 = \mathbf{₹5,000}$
  4. Net Eligible Common Credit ($C_3$) = $C_2 - (D_1 + D_2) = 1,00,000 - 25,000 = \mathbf{₹75,000}$

Amount to be added to Output Tax Liability in GSTR-3B = $D_1 + D_2 = \mathbf{₹25,000}$.
